# The Money Hunt (1986) — unsolved, $1,000,000 never paid

An armchair treasure hunt by John Poser: a 150-piece jigsaw, a fold-out poster (North
America map on one side, a page-order flow chart on the other), and a 32-page clue
book. The answer form asks for a **State/Province** and "**Specifically at** ___",
anywhere in the US or Canada. The prize expired 31 March 1988 after a decade of
litigation between Poser and a former collaborator ("Mr. S") who alone knew the
answer and held it ransom; the answer was never published. Karen Puzzles' 2021 video
and her shared scans (links in `docs/sources.md`) are the reason anyone still works on
it.

## What is established

- **Page 12 = WASHINGTON**, solved by Karen's sister Katie in November 2021 by
  counting each bird species and indexing the count into the species name. This is
  the only proven page and the template for what a solve looks like.
- **The creator's son** told Karen the solution involves the **28 July 1945 B-25
  bomber crash into the Empire State Building**.
- **Karen's acrostic**, one letter per page in poster reading order, reads
  **EMPIRE STATE BUILDING NY USA** (24 letters; a trailing L is uncertain).
- Reading order (poster flow chart): 15, 9, 18, 24, 14, 6, 17, 21, 23, 27, 19, 7, 5,
  8, 10, 25, 20, 22, 28, 13, 16, 26, 12, 11.
- The working theory: each of the 24 reading-order pages hides one US state or
  Canadian province.

## Notes from 2026-10-05

1. **A full image-level state hunt** over all 24 pages — `docs/money-hunt-state-analysis.md`.
   Results: 2 high-confidence states (p12 WASHINGTON proven; **p14 FLORIDA**, an
   unmistakable state outline with a hurricane on it), 7 medium reads (p27 LOUISIANA
   via a JAZZ sign; p24 ONTARIO via five-pin bowling, a Canadian invention; p23
   KENTUCKY horse show vs TENNESSEE via crossword clue 145; p22 KENTUCKY polo;
   p10 CONNECTICUT via clue 8 "A Connecticut Yankee"; p25 NEW YORK via clues 28/62/104;
   p11 IDAHO via clue 55 "Belonging to the Potato State"), and 15 pages with no
   resolvable state signal.
2. **An errata for the earlier page transcriptions** —
   `docs/money-hunt-transcription-errata.md`. Fourteen defects, including three pages
   whose transcription describes an entirely different scene. Those page files are
   now short stubs. The image read in `docs/money-hunt-state-analysis.md` is the
   working copy until the pages are transcribed again. The errata is also a caution
   about AI-vision transcription of dense artwork.
3. **Scripted micro-analyses** — `results/money_hunt_micro_analysis.md`: anagram tests
   of every deliberate misspelling against all state/province/place names (no clean
   hits; AWITHIAKNANNAI merely *contains* HAWAII's letters with eight left over),
   calculator-upside-down readings of the page-28 prices (5537→LESS and 618→BIG
   reproduce; 334→HEE, 16M→WGI, 149145→SHIGHI do not), and index tests of every number
   cluster against the alphabet, the acrostic, admission order and alphabetical state
   lists (nothing compelling).
4. **A negative result on the acrostic.** No rule was found that maps page → state →
   acrostic letter consistently. The only rule reproducing page 12's S (letter at
   index = digit-sum of the folio) fails on page 14 and page 27; first-letter,
   postal-letter and any-index rules fail everywhere tested. Either the acrostic is
   not derived from the per-page states, or most per-page states are still wrong.
   This is flagged, not forced.

## What would move it forward

- **600-dpi re-renders** of the 17 regions listed in the state analysis (bead letters
  on p5, clock hands on p9, the letter columns on p18, the suitcase glyphs on p10, the
  three-card pips on p26 vs p11, the p28 spine titles, and others). These are the
  carriers most likely to hold the remaining states.
- **Re-transcription** of the fifteen pages named in the errata, from high-resolution
  renders, before any further page-level reasoning.
- **The three complete crossword clue lists** now transcribed (p11: 38–69, p22: 73–117,
  p23: 121–147) solved as crosswords; their grids are the top-hat shape on p25 and the
  answers may index into the states.
- Honest framing: the r/themoneyhunt community's objection stands — if Mr. S embedded
  incomplete information, no amount of analysis yields a unique answer. The 1945 crash
  theme and the ESB acrostic are the strongest structural leads; the per-page states
  are the strongest mechanical ones; they have not yet been reconciled.
