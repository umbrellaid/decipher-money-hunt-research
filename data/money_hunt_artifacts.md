## 1. Jigsaw puzzle (150 pieces, full color)

Image: the box-front collage art (vintage car, biplane "B-HE", roulette wheel,
dancers, safari figures, golf bag, safe full of gold, "THE MONEY HUNT / REWARD:
$1,000,000" banner) on a green background. Text is printed in the green border
and below the image.

### Poem (below the picture, in white capitals)

> A SAD STORY WAS TOLD, OF ONE NOT VERY OLD.
> CLIMB WAY UP HIGH, NOT QUITE TO THE SKY.
> WITH LIVES MADE TO PAY, FIND THE PLACE ONE DID PLAY.

(Exactly three lines; nothing further is cut off in the photo.)

### Border text (white, rotated, reads around the border in one continuous passage)

> Now that you have assembled the jigsaw puzzle, this poem will help you find
> the treasure… We recommend that you read the poem on the bottom of the
> puzzle several times. Now that we have started you on the right treasure-hunt
> path, turn to page 11, the pool page, where people are playing cards… One of
> the cards is the queen of spades – what does that mean? Look for other clues
> on the same page as well as all other drawings. If all else fails, read the
> instructions.

### Significance

- The jigsaw is the hunt's **entry point**: it names page 11 (pool page,
  playing cards, queen of spades) as the first place to go and explicitly
  teaches the per-page deduction method ("Look for other clues on the same
  page as well as all other drawings").
- It carries the **only copy of the poem**, which describes the final mystery
  location (see §5 analysis).

## 2. Poster — "clue poster" side (flip side of the map)

A winding stippled trail (dark, speckled bands on white) with a small
black-and-white thumbnail of **each of the 24 clue pages (5–28)** placed along
it. Title top right: "THE MONEY HUNT REWARD: $1,000,000". A **START flag** is
planted at one end of the trail (next to the page-15 thumbnail, top left); the
other end terminates in an **arrow pointing at a pile of money bags** (bottom
right, next to the page-11 thumbnail).

A later owner annotated the poster with large blue numbers identifying each
thumbnail's book page number (5–28, each exactly once).

### The trail order (START flag → money arrow)

Reading the thumbnails along the band from the START flag to the arrow:

| 1 | **15** | 10 | 21 | 19 | 20 |

This is **exactly the "poster path order" of the
[wishkohaku mapping](money_hunt_wishkohaku_mapping.md)** (pages 15 and 9 each
supply two letters, matching the doubled first two stops). Every consecutive
pair was checked against the band connectivity in the photos (15–9, 9–18,
18–24, 24–14, 14–6, 6–17, 17–21, 21–23, 23–27, 27–7, 7–19, 19–5, 5–8, 8–10,

least-photographically-certain link is 19–5 (long diagonal across the middle).

### Open question — direction of travel

The jigsaw says the hunt *starts* at page 11, but 11 sits at the **arrow end**
of the trail (next to the treasure), while the flag marked START sits at page
15. Resolutions: (a) the flag marks the start of the trail drawing, and the
jigsaw's "turn to page 11" is a tutorial step — the worked example (queen of
spades) that teaches the method, after which the actual route begins at the
poster START (page 15); (b) the trail is walked arrow→flag. The fact that the
flag→arrow order reproduces the mapping that yields a form-fitting answer
supports (a) with the flag→arrow read.

### Queen-of-spades check (native-res, 2026-10-10)

Page 11's umbrella table carries: **one face card in spades (the queen of
spades — confirming the jigsaw's claim)**, a **3 of spades**, one face-down
card being held, and a fan of face-down cards in the woman's hand. Candidate
meanings for "what does that mean?":
- **Queen = 12 → page 12.** Simplest reading; page 12 is the poster-trail
  neighbor of 11, supporting a visit chain jigsaw → 11 → 12 → … walked
  arrow→flag, while the *letter* order remains flag→arrow (locked by the
  verified letters 13→L, 28→I — letters are presumably assembled into the
  answer form's order at the end).
- Pallas/Athena → owl: **not supported** — the bottom-right of the (rotated)
  page-12 collage is a hen and a cage with two parrots, no owl.
- "Curse of Scotland" / Hearts' 13-point Black Lady: possible but point to
  nothing checkable yet.
So the queen of spades most plausibly demonstrates the method: object →
association (queen = 12) → next page (12).

## 5. Analysis — what the artifacts settle

**Page sequencing (the thing "the author didn't know what to make of").** The
24 clue pages are not meant to be read in numeric order, and the poster is not
decorative: the poster trail *is* the page order, running from the START flag
(page 15) through all 24 thumbnails to page 11 at the treasure arrow. The
jigsaw is the on-ramp: assemble it → read the poem → go to page 11 to learn the
deduction method from the queen-of-spades worked example → follow the poster
trail, taking one deduction (one "subtraction") per page.

**The poem describes the final location.** Three candidate reads:
1. **King Kong riddle → Empire State Building.** A sad story told of one not
   very old (Kong); climb way up high, not quite to the sky (Kong's climb);
   with lives made to pay (the deaths); find the place one did play (where the
   story played out). Strong fit for lines 1–2.
2. **Empire State Building, plain.** "Climb way up high" = skyscraper
   observation deck; "lives made to pay" = the July 28, 1945 B-25 bomber crash
   (14 deaths) — note the p.5 clock hands sit at ~7:28, a known motif.
3. Something else entirely (the poem remains ambiguous — "the place one did
   play" could be a theater/stadium reading).

**Impact on the wishkohaku mapping: substantially rehabilitated.**
- Its page order is now confirmed to be a *real, physical* order (the poster
  trail), not an arbitrary one — every adjacency checks out.
- Its output message "US NY THE EMPIRE STATE BUILDING" fits the registration
  form's three blanks (COUNTRY / STATE / SPECIFICALLY AT) exactly, and the
  poem is at least partially consistent with that answer.
- What remains unproven is the per-page *letter extraction* (only 13→L and
  28→I verified; two comment-sourced pairs were falsified). The mapping's
  weakest point used to be the "US NY" double-letter pages 15/9 — but those are
  now the two stops at the START flag, which plausibly each yield two letters
  (the country and state are short chunks).
- Falsifiable next checks: pages 15 and 9 should each yield two clean
  subtractions (U,S and N,Y); page 11 (last stop, → G) should yield "G".

**Other notes.**
- The answer form's "specifically at ___" implies the final answer is a named
  place, consistent with a building — not coordinates.
- Rules (book p.2–3): 3 guesses per purchase; location in the US or Canada;
  deadline March 31, 1988; "it is possible that there could be more than one
  clue per page — you have been forewarned" — consistent with pages carrying
  both a chain clue and a letter deduction.
- Box ©1988 / Touche Ross letter dated 1986 / book ©1985 — mixed printings.
