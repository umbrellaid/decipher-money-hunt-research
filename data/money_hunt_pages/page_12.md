### WWII US Navy ship-namesake check (`@BiffEnterprises`, tested 2026-11-11)
- **Claim:** *"all 10 species of bird share names of WWII era US Naval ships!!"* (proposed as
  a military tie-in to *USS Washington* `BB-56`).
- **Historical check (DANFS):** Most pictured birds do match WWII US Navy hulls — *USS Cockatoo*
  (`AMc-8`), *USS Hornbill* (`AMc-13` / `AMS-19`) and *USS Toucan* (`AM-387`), *USS Flamingo*
  (`AM-32` / `AMS-11`), *USS Penguin* (`AM-33` / `ASR-12`), *USS Macaw* (`ASR-11`) and *USS Parakeet*
  (`AMc-34`), *USS Pigeon* (`ASR-6` / `AM-374`) and *USS Crane* (`DD-109`), *USS Heron* (`AM-10`),
  *USS Pelican* (`AM-27`), *USS Ibis* (`AM-134`), and *USS Humming Bird* (`AMc-26`). However:
  1. **Exceptions exist** depending on species ID: *USS Spoonbill* (`MSC-202`) was commissioned in
     **1955** (post-WWII), *USS Sunbird* (`ASR-15`) in **1950**, *USS Wren* (`DD-568`) was named for
     USMC Sgt. Solomon Wren rather than the bird, and there was no WWII *USS Cockatiel* or *USS Kiwi*.
  2. **High base-rate artifact:** The US Navy named hundreds of WWI/WWII *Lapwing*-, *Auk*-, and
     *Accentor*-class minesweepers (`AM` / `AMc` / `AMS`) and *Pigeon*-class submarine rescue ships
     (`ASR`) systematically after nearly every common English bird name. Any random 10-bird aviary
     list hits 7–9 WWII minesweepers automatically, so this is naval naming coverage rather than an
     independent puzzle layer.
