## Page 1B — "Decipher II game pays off in cold cash"

*By Tom Forstrom, Of the Statesman-Journal*

> An investment of $12 has paid $6,250 to a Salem woman, who received the
> "cold hard cash" Monday enclosed in a giant ice cube.
>
> Eileen Novak won the money by solving one of the four coded messages in a
> game called Decipher II. Three other people from around the country also
> solved the clues and received the same amount of money.
>
> Novak, who works part time at Bethel Elementary School, bought the $12 game
> in August and worked on it off and on until mid-February. On Valentine's
> Day, when she was home sick with the flu, she figured out the answer.
>
> Decipher II is played by first putting together a double-sided jigsaw
> puzzle made up entirely of rows of numbers. A booklet gives clues to
> deciphering the coded messages of the game. The numbers in the messages
> represent letters, much like in cryptograms.
>
> But it isn't easy. There are books to read, passages to find and dead ends
> involved in the game.
>
> One clue, for example, involved 11 authors. Not only did the puzzle solvers
> have to find the right author and piece of literature, they had to find the
> right passage in the book, poem or other work.
>
> Another clue was simply "492," Novak said. She finally found that it
> referred to a passage in "Gulliver's Travels." The solved message was a
> poem — based on the "Gulliver's Travels" passage — about the cartoon strip
> "Bloom County."
>
> Novak said when she discovered "Bloom County" was involved, she bought a
> book of the cartoons by
>
> *Turn to Decipher, Page 3B.*

## Page 3B — "Decipher— Continued from Page 1B."

> Berke Breathed to make sure the names were spelled correctly.
>
> The 400-pound block of ice, carved in the shape of a dollar sign, was

>
> Novak also received a fruit basket — complete with champagne. The ice

> Novak said the only help she got was when she was trying to figure out a
> clue by following what she thought was a good lead on author John
> Steinbeck. Her 15-year-old son, Ken, told her to pick the least logical
> author.
>
> Clues also were given on a special Decipher II hotline.
>
> This game still has $75,000 to give away, since three unsolved messages
> remain in the jigsaw puzzle.
>
> Games and puzzles interest Novak.
>
> "I don't like TV," she said, "so this is something to do in the evening."
>
> She does crossword puzzles and likes Scrabble.
>
> "I never tried to do codes or ciphers before," she said.
>
> Novak and her husband, Kenneth, also have a 19-year-old daughter, Jennifer,

> and the impending delivery of her "cold hard cash," she said. Some of the

>
> The experience of breaking the code was a satisfying payoff for Novak. She

## Facts established/confirmed by this article

- Winner: **Eileen Novak** of Salem, OR (confirms the Q6 name correction —
  not "Barbara Novak"); part-time worker at Bethel Elementary School; husband
  Kenneth, son Ken (15), daughter Jennifer (19).
- **Four winners** for 2-1 ("three other people … received the same amount"),
  $6,250 each = $250 cash + $6,000 check, delivered inside a **400-lb block
  of ice carved as a dollar sign**, plus a fruit basket with champagne;
  delivery Monday 1986-03-24 (article is Tuesday 03-25).
- Timeline: bought the game **August 1985**; solved **Valentine's Day 1986**
  (home sick with the flu).
- **$75,000 remained** across three unsolved messages at that date.
- Decipher II had its own **clue hotline**.
- Solving heuristic that broke it: her son's advice to "pick the **least
  logical author**" — she had been pursuing a John Steinbeck lead; the true
  author was Jonathan Swift. (Rhymes with Baldwin & Sherman's report that
  Holland designed clues with multiple interpretation levels and "the obvious
  reading is usually wrong".)
- The plaintext poem was **about "Bloom County"** (Berke Breathed), based on
  the *Gulliver's Travels* passage; she cross-checked name spellings against
  a Bloom County book.
- Warren Holland explicitly named as creator of the game.
